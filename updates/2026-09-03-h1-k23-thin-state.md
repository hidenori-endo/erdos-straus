# h=1, k=23: non-pure miss states

For `h=1`,

`N=(p+3)/4=1+210t`, `C_23=N+5=6(35t+1)`.

The seed is 6. Modulo 23,

```text
Q      = {1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12,13,16,18}
NR     = {5,7,10,11,14,15,17,19,20,21,22}
D_seed= {1,2,3,4,6,9,12,13,18}
t1     = 17
```

Exhausting parity-aware minimal boxes gives exactly three non-pure miss states.

For E=1:

```text
(r,m,c) = (5,1,7), (14,1,15)
```

For E=2:

```text
(r1,r2,m,c) = (5,14,1,6)
```

For E=3, every state hits. Consequently a non-pure miss has at most two NR prime-factor units, and its NR residues are forced to be 5 and/or 14; the QR part has product 1 modulo 23.

Among the 4,810 `h=1` primes below `1e8` surviving the base shifts `k=3,7,15`:

```text
k=23 hit              3322
k=23 miss             1488
  pure miss           1424
  non-pure thin miss    64
```

The 64 thin states split as E=1 residue 5 (26), E=1 residue 14 (32), and E=2 residues 5 and 14 (6).

Examples:

```text
p=21841:   C23=6*911,       911=14 mod23
p=972721:  C23=6*40531,     40531=5 mod23
p=7071121: C23=6*37*7963,  residues 14,5 mod23
```

Since `C_47=C_23+6=6(R+1)`, this is a natural entry point for a consecutive-cofactor argument. But among the 64 thin cases, only 36 hit at `k=47` and 28 still miss. Therefore `k=23 thin miss => k=47 hit` is false.
