# 2026-09-04: inverse escape lemma and retro-capture conjecture

This continuation tries to prove the pointwise cross-layer low-escape statement suggested
by the complement-normalization audit.  The full statement is not proved.  Two useful
results do emerge:

1. a general character-or-sparse-support inverse lemma;
2. a precise **retro-capture conjecture** which passes the complete finite audit and is
   strictly cross-layer: high escape occurs in individual layers, but every observed case
   has already been covered by an earlier layer.

## 1. A general inverse support lemma

Write a finite abelian group multiplicatively, let `j` be a nonidentity target, and let
`g_1,...,g_s` be a sequence whose subset products miss `j`.  Let `A` be the set of its
distinct nonidentity terms and put `H=<A>`.

> **Lemma 12 (character or sparse support).**  One of the following holds:
>
> 1. `j notin H`, so the miss is a character/subgroup obstruction;
> 2. `|A|<=4`;
> 3. `j in H` and `2|A|<|H|`.

**Proof.**  Suppose `j in H` and `|A|>=5`.  The subset products using each member of `A`
at most once are contained among the sequence's subset products, so they also miss `j`.
The corrected distinct-class subset-sum theorem gives

~~~
|Sigma(A)| >= min(|H|, 2|A|)
~~~

because `A` generates `H`, excludes the identity, and has at least five elements.  Since
`Sigma(A)` misses `j in H`, it is not all of `H`; therefore `2|A|<|H|`.  QED.

For a layer, `j=-1`, `H` is generated by the nonidentity prime-factor residues of
`p+4e`, and identity-class factors may be discarded.  Thus every miss is either a zero-
escape odd-character obstruction or has nonidentity residue support strictly below half
of its generated subgroup (apart from the explicit support-at-most-four branch).

This is a genuine general inverse lemma, but it is weaker than a constant escape bound:
it controls the number of residue classes, not their multiplicities.

## 2. The sharp small-support obstruction

The normalized cyclic counterexample can be strengthened.  In `C_(2m)=<g>`, take `m-1`
copies of `g` and `m-1` copies of `g^(-1)`.  Its subset exponents fill

~~~
-(m-1), ..., -1, 0, 1, ..., m-1  (mod 2m),
~~~

which is every element except `m`; hence the subset products miss `-1=g^m`.  Any subgroup
containing either `g` or `g^(-1)` contains `g^m`, so every subgroup avoiding `-1` excludes
all `2m-2` terms.  Therefore

~~~
minimum normalized escape = 2m-2 = |C_(2m)|-2.
~~~

The general stabilizer bound is thus essentially sharp even with only **two** nonidentity
residue classes.  `code/escape_counterexample.cpp` asserts this family, as well as the
one-class family, for every tested `2<=m<=31`.

This rules out an inverse theorem saying merely that high escape forces many distinct
residue classes.  The viable analytic dichotomy must instead exploit that very small
support confines every nonidentity prime factor to very few progressions.

## 3. The retro-capture conjecture

For a missed layer define the normalized escape

~~~
b_(d,e)(p) = min_{chi odd modulo 4d} Omega_{chi(q) != 1}(p+4e).
~~~

The finite data suggest the following stronger and cleaner statement than a positive-
proportion lemma.

> **Retro-capture conjecture.**  If a hard prime `p` misses layer `(d,e)` and
> `b_(d,e)(p)>=3`, then `p` hits some Type-II layer `(d',e')` with `d'<d`.

If true, every prime surviving all layers through `D` has escape at most two in **every**
missed layer through `D`.

This was checked for every hard prime `11<=p<10^8` and every layer through `D=32`.
There is no high-escape case among any sequential survivor.  High escape is nevertheless
common enough in individual layers to make the test nonvacuous:

| audited layer | misses with `b>=3` | maximum `b` | latest first-hit `d'` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `(17,17)` | 881 | 4 | 12 |
| `(17,289)` | 176 | 4 | 5 |
| `(19,19)` | 259 | 4 | 6 |
| `(23,23)` | 3,392 | 5 | 7 |
| `(29,29)` | 838 | 4 | 6 |
| `(31,31)` | 1,341 | 6 | 6 |

For example, the 881 high-escape misses of `(17,17)` split by their first earlier hit as

~~~
d'=1:403, 2:318, 3:92, 4:42, 5:12, 6:8, 7:1, 9:1, 10:3, 12:1.
~~~

These counts come from the single-layer mode of `progressive_escape.cpp`; that mode does
not condition the audited layer on earlier misses, and then reports where each high-
escape case was first caught.  They are finite computations, not a proof of the
conjecture.

## 4. Why the general proof did not close

Lemma 12 gives a useful split, but neither branch currently forces an earlier hit.

- In the small-support branch, multiplicities can make escape almost `|G|` while all
  nonidentity prime factors occupy only one or two residue classes.  This is strongly
  siftable but is not pointwise impossible.
- In the larger-support branch, `2|A|<|H|` supplies a positive missing density, but the
  number and structure of possible incomplete supports still need a uniform majorant.
- The identity `gcd(p+4e,p+4e') | 4(e-e')` separates prime factors larger than `D^2`, but
  says nothing strong enough about repeated small factors or the unfactored cofactor.

The closest inverse subset-sum theorems describe zero-free sequences or large incomplete
sets.  They do not turn a single prescribed missing element plus robust generation into
an earlier shifted divisor.  In particular, the two-class cyclic family satisfies all
the finite-group hypotheses one might try to use.

Thus no unconditional derivation of retro-capture was found.  Constructing an arithmetic
counterexample is also nontrivial: forcing the two-class factorization of `p+4e` while
keeping the shared `p` prime and making all earlier shifts miss leads to simultaneous
prime/factorization conditions beyond Dirichlet's theorem.

## 5. Consequence if retro-capture is proved

The fixed-budget sieve's total permitted escape would improve from

~~~
B_D = sum_{d<=D} 2^omega(d)(phi(4d)-1)
~~~

to simply `2L(D)` on the sequential residual.  Together with complement normalization,
every residual prime would be covered by odd-character conditions

~~~
chi(-1)=-1,
Omega_{chi(q)!=1}(p+4e)<=2.
~~~

This removes the dominant `B_D/L(D) approximately D` loss.  A naive union over all odd
characters still costs

~~~
C_D = product_{d<=D}(phi(4d)/2)^(2^omega(d)),
~~~

so retro-capture alone does not finish the Erdős--Straus proof.  The next analytic step
would have to sum characters according to their order rather than pay `C_D` in full, and
control those character sums uniformly in growing `d`.

The evidence therefore supports retro-capture as the right combinatorial lemma, while
Lemma 12 supplies a proved fallback for constructing that order-sensitive majorant.  The
universal pointwise statement remains explicitly conjectural.
