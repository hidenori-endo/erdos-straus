# 2026-09-04: proof-route audit and the exponent-2 layer sieve

The two routes left in the previous notes were compared by (i) ability to reach an
individual-prime contradiction, (ii) amount already proved, and (iii) compatibility with
the finite data.  The best-supported route is the **growing identity-layer sieve with
character separation**.  The least-NR mass route, in its present form,
does not have inequalities in the direction needed for a contradiction.

Reproduction code: `code/exponent2_layers.cpp`; the additional NR-mass columns are in
`code/survivor_nr.cpp`.

## 1. Correction: there are nine exponent-2 layers

For `d in {1,2,3,6}`, the pairs `(d,e)` with `d(e)=d` are

~~~
(1,1),
(2,2), (2,4),
(3,3), (3,9),
(6,6), (6,12), (6,18), (6,36).
~~~

Thus the total is `1+2+2+4 = 9`, not 8 as stated in the previous layer note.  All six hard
classes are `1 (mod 24)`, so Theorem 3 becomes particularly simple on these layers:

> The layer `(d,e)` hits iff `p+4e` has a divisor congruent to `-1 (mod 4d)`.

Because every group `(Z/4d Z)^*` here has exponent two, this is also equivalent to `-1`
belonging to the subgroup generated by the prime-factor residues of `p+4e`.

## 2. A provable sieve bound

Consider only the weaker, single-prime certificate

~~~
q | p+4e,      q == -1 (mod 4d).
~~~

It is a valid divisor certificate from Theorem 3.  Consequently, a prime that fails all
nine exact layers must avoid every such certificate.

For a sifting prime `q>36`, the forbidden residues `p == -4e (mod q)` are distinct.  Their
number depends only on `q mod 24`:

| `q mod 24` | 1 | 5 | 7 | 11 | 13 | 17 | 19 | 23 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| layer residues `nu(q)` | 0 | 0 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 9 |

The average over the eight reduced residue classes is

~~~
(0+0+3+3+0+0+1+9)/8 = 2.
~~~

The residue `p == 0 (mod q)` imposing primality is distinct from these for `q>36`, so the
combined sieve has dimension `1+2=3`.  The prime number theorem/Mertens theorem in the
fixed progressions modulo 24 gives

~~~
sum_{q<z} (1+nu(q))/q = 3 log log z + O(1).
~~~

For squarefree products of sifting primes, the Chinese remainder theorem gives the exact
local density `prod_q(1+nu(q))/q`, with the usual `O(prod_q(1+nu(q)))` interval remainder.
More explicitly, write `p=h+840t`, remove the `1+nu(q)` residue classes of `t mod q`, and
apply the upper-bound sieve with (for example) `z=x^(1/4)`.  The sum of the elementary
interval remainders is `O(z (log z)^O(1))`, hence negligible beside `x/(log x)^3`; primes
`p<=z` contribute only another negligible term.  The fixed-dimension Selberg upper-bound
sieve therefore proves:

> **Theorem 4.**  For each hard class `h`, the number of primes `p<=x`,
> `p == h (mod 840)`, which fail all nine exponent-2 layers is
> `O_h(x/(log x)^3)`.

Equivalently, among hard primes their relative proportion is `O((log x)^-2)`.  Exact layer
failures are a subset of the single-prime-sieve survivors, so using products of prime
factors can only improve this bound.

This is a direct application of the standard upper-bound sieve (for example,
[Ford, *Sieve Methods*, Theorem 2.4](https://ford126.web.illinois.edu/sieve2023.pdf)); the
only nonstandard input is the displayed `nu(q)` table.  It proves the unconditional
logarithmic saving requested at the end of the previous layer note.  It is a density
theorem, not an Erdős--Straus proof: the bound does not show that the remaining thin set is
finite or empty.

## 3. Exact character separation: dimension `11/2`

The exact subgroup condition gives a stronger theorem without a bilinear assumption.  Let
`H` be the subgroup generated by the prime-factor residues of `p+4e`.  In the exponent-two
group `G=(Z/4d Z)^*`,

~~~
-1 not in H
  iff there is a character psi:G->{+1,-1}
      with psi(-1)=-1 and psi(H)=+1.
~~~

This is the elementary separation of a vector subspace from a point outside it.  Thus a
layer fails iff, for one of the odd real characters `psi`, **every** prime factor `q` of
`p+4e` has `psi(q)=+1`.  The number of choices is:

| modulus | 4 | 8 | 12 | 24 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| odd real characters | 1 | 2 | 2 | 4 |

Across the nine layers there are `1 * 2^2 * 2^2 * 4^4 = 4096` character assignments.
Fix one assignment.  For a sifting prime `q>36`, layer `i` excludes the distinct residue
`p == -4e_i (mod q)` exactly when `psi_i(q)=-1`.  Every odd nonprincipal real character is
negative on half of the eight reduced classes modulo 24.  Hence the mean number of layer
residues is `9/2`; adding the primality residue gives dimension `11/2`.  Applying the same
upper-bound sieve to each of the finitely many assignments proves:

> **Theorem 5.**  For each hard class `h`, the number of primes `p<=x`,
> `p == h (mod 840)`, which fail all nine exact exponent-2 layers is
> `O_h(x/(log x)^(11/2))`.

Among hard primes the relative proportion is therefore `O((log x)^(-9/2))`.  This
strictly strengthens Theorem 4.  The code enumerates the odd characters and finds zero
character/subgroup mismatches through `10^8`; the equivalence itself is finite-group
linear algebra and is not dependent on that computation.

## 4. Finite check and pre-asymptotic size

`code/exponent2_layers.cpp` gives:

| range | hard primes | exact common failures | rate | prime-atom survivors | rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `[10^6,10^7)` | 18,143 | 499 | 0.027504 | 885 | 0.048779 |
| `[10^7,10^8)` | 158,955 | 2,853 | 0.017948 | 5,783 | 0.036381 |

The atom-survivor rate changes by a factor `0.746`, consistent with the proved relative
dimension 2.  The exact-failure rate changes by `0.653`, which corresponds empirically to
about three powers of `log` relative to hard primes at this range.  Theorem 5 gives the
stronger asymptotic upper-bound exponent `9/2`; the gap is compatible with the large union
constant (4096 character assignments) and the small logarithmic range.  Thus the fitted
exponent is only a finite-range diagnostic, not the theorem's asymptotic exponent.

## 5. Verdict on the least-NR mass route

The least-NR lemma itself survives the audit.  Among the atlas survivors below `10^7`,
there are 134 rank-zero adaptive misses.  Of these, 14 have `k<r`, and all 14 have
external-NR valuation at least two.  There are no violations.

But two facts prevent the lemma from being a completion mechanism as currently stated.

1. The hypothesis `k<r` covers only 14 of those 134 rank-zero misses.  Moreover 32 of the
   134 misses have total external-NR valuation exactly one.  Later ranks also continue to
   have many `E=1` misses; repeated adaptive misses do not force valuation growth in a
   single `C_k`.
2. `C>=r^E` gives a lower bound for `p` in terms of `r`.  A standard least-nonresidue bound
   gives an upper bound for `r` in terms of `p`; these inequalities are compatible.  For
   fixed `E`, combining `r <= p^theta` with `C>=r^E` cannot yield a contradiction.  One
   would need `E` growing past `log p/log r`, or a lower bound/product statement for many
   NR primes dividing the same `C_k`.  Neither is presently forced, and the finite data
   points the other way.

This rejects only the proposed **mass plus least-NR-upper-bound proof mechanism**, not the
empirical adaptive external-NR conjecture itself.

## 6. Route to test next

The next proof-relevant checkpoint is now precise:

1. extend character separation to the exponent `>2` layers, where bounded
   multiplicities mean that the divisor residues are no longer a subgroup;
2. prove that the resulting effective sieve dimension `kappa(D)` tends to infinity and
   control the union/majorant constants quantitatively;
3. obtain estimates uniform in a slowly growing budget `D=D(x)`, so that the sieve
   dimension grows rather than remaining fixed;
4. combine that uniform estimate with an explicit finite verification.  If the uniform
   constants are not strong enough to make the exceptional count less than one, a
   parity-breaking or other non-sieve argument will still be needed for the residual set.

This route uses the exact closed form of Theorem 3 and explains both the observed shallow
certificates and the increasing sieve dimension.  Unlike quotient transport, it now has
the concrete exact-layer Theorem 5 and a falsifiable next target: unbounded dimension with
uniform constants.
