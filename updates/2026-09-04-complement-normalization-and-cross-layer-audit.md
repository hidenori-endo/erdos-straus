# 2026-09-04: complement normalization and cross-layer audit

This update directly tests the proposed cross-layer low-escape lemma.  A previously
unused symmetry materially improves its formulation: replacing a divisor by its
complement normalizes every layer target to `-1`.  The resulting escape is measured only
against odd characters and is smaller than the unnormalized escape in some cases.

The verdict is:

- the normalization, odd-character cover, deletion interpretation, and large-factor
  disjointness across shifts are proved;
- normalized escape is at most two in every simultaneous survivor tested through
  `p<10^8` and `D<=24`;
- no proof of an absolute bound or a positive low-escape proportion is obtained;
- a cyclic counterexample still rules out any proof using one layer's finite-group state
  alone.

Reproduction code: the revised `code/progressive_escape.cpp`,
`code/escape_counterexample.cpp`, and `code/sieve_budget.cpp`.

## 1. Complement normalization

> **Lemma 9 (fixed target).**  Put `m=4d` and `n=p+4e`, where `p` is a hard prime,
> `d=d(e)`, and `p` does not divide `d` (in particular, this holds when `p>D` and
> `d<=D`).  Then
>
> ~~~
> exists k|n with k == -n (mod m)
>   iff exists l|n with l == -1 (mod m).
> ~~~

**Proof.**  Since `d|e`, `n == p (mod m)`; the hypothesis gives `gcd(n,m)=1`.
Complementation is an involution on the divisors of `n`.  If `l=n/k`, then

~~~
k == -n (mod m)  iff  l=n/k == -1 (mod m).
~~~

The converse is the same calculation with `k=n/l`.  QED.

`progressive_escape.cpp` computes both targets independently and asserts their hit/miss
equivalence for every layer examined before (and including) the first hit.  The assertion
passed in the full run on 179,468 hard primes `11<=p<10^8` through `D=32`.

This removes two artificial complications from the previous sieve formulation:

1. the target no longer depends on `p`;
2. the separating characters are exactly the odd characters `chi(-1)=-1`.

## 2. Exact odd-character escape

List the prime factors of `n` with multiplicity as `g_1,...,g_s` in
`G=(Z/4dZ)^*`, and define

~~~
b(n;d) = min_{H<=G, -1 notin H} #{i:g_i notin H}.
~~~

Lemma 8 now specializes to

> **Corollary 9.1.**
>
> ~~~
> b(n;d) = min_{chi odd modulo 4d} Omega_{chi(q) != 1}(n).
> ~~~

There are exactly `phi(4d)/2` odd characters: evaluation at `-1` maps the character
group onto `{+1,-1}`, since the character modulo 4 supplies an odd character.  Hence the
assignment budget through `D` improves to

~~~
C_D = product_{d<=D} (phi(4d)/2)^(2^omega(d)),
log C_D <= L(D) log(2D).
~~~

The target-normalizing residue-class split in the proof of Theorem 7 is no longer needed.
Uniform estimates for primes in the changing character moduli are still needed.

There is also an exact robustness interpretation.

> **Lemma 10 (deletion distance).**  `b(n;d)` is the minimum number of prime-factor
> occurrences that must be deleted from the factor sequence so that `-1` is no longer in
> the subgroup generated by the remaining occurrences.

**Proof.**  Given `H` avoiding `-1`, delete the terms outside `H`; the retained terms
generate a subgroup inside `H`.  Conversely, after any deletion for which the retained
terms generate a subgroup `H` avoiding `-1`, every nondeleted term lies in `H`, so the
number deleted is at least the subgroup escape.  Taking both minima proves equality.
QED.

Thus `b>=3` means that `-1` remains generated after deletion of any two factor
occurrences, even though the bounded-multiplicity subset products themselves miss `-1`.
This isolates the precise inverse subset-product configuration that a cross-layer proof
would have to control.

## 3. Finite cross-layer proposition

The revised program enumerates every subgroup of `(Z/4dZ)^*`, so its reported escape is
the exact minimum above rather than the stabilizer upper bound.

> **Finite proposition.**  For every hard prime `11<=p<10^8`, every `1<=D<=24`, and every
> layer `(d,e)` with `d<=D`, if `p` misses every layer through `D`, then
> `b(p+4e;d)<=2`.

The selected survivor rows are:

| `D` | survivors | character misses (`b=0`) | multiplicity misses | maximum `b` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 4,771 | 32,101 | 1,296 | 2 |
| 8 | 247 | 3,903 | 296 | 2 |
| 12 | 16 | 427 | 37 | 2 |
| 16 | 3 | 109 | 14 | 2 |
| 18 | 2 | 84 | 10 | 2 |
| 24 | 1 | 59 | 8 | 2 |
| 25 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The program checks every intermediate `D`, not only the displayed rows.  The three primes
surviving `D=16` are caught as follows:

| `p` | first later hit |
|---:|---:|
| 3,361 | `(25,125)` |
| 4,722,169 | `(24,96)` |
| 24,887,641 | `(18,108)` |

For `p=3361`, the old target `-(p+4e)` gave escape three at `(17,17)`.  The equivalent
fixed target `-1` gives escape one.  This explains and retracts the apparent empirical
counterexample in the preceding audit.

As a primality control, the same computation was run on all 47,770 hard-class integers
`11<=p<10^7` that are coprime to every `d<=32`.  The maximum normalized escape on every
simultaneous survivor was again two.  At `D=32`, 471 composite survivors remain and all
their misses are character obstructions (`b=0`); the first examples are squares such as
`37^2,41^2,43^2,47^2`.  This control suggests that primality alone is not the source of
the observed small escape.  It is still only a finite computation.

## 4. What fixed differences prove

> **Lemma 11 (large-factor disjointness).**  For distinct shifts `e,e'<=D^2`, any prime
> dividing both `p+4e` and `p+4e'` divides `4(e'-e)`.  In particular an odd common prime
> is at most `|e'-e|<D^2`.

**Proof.**  A common divisor divides the difference
`(p+4e')-(p+4e)=4(e'-e)`.  An odd prime divisor therefore divides `e'-e`.  QED.

Thus prime factors larger than `D^2` belong to at most one shifted integer.  This is the
exact deterministic independence supplied by the fixed differences.  It does **not**
prove the proposed low-escape lemma:

- escape factors can be small or repeated;
- even if all escape factors were large and distinct across layers, the elementary
  product bound only limits their number by `O(log x/log D)` per layer, not by a constant;
- the condition that the remaining cofactor does not create a `-1` divisor is not encoded
  by pairwise gcds.

Consequently the fixed-difference lemma is useful input to a sieve, but is insufficient
as a pointwise contradiction.

## 5. Why the pointwise conjecture remains open

The normalized cyclic family still gives an unbounded one-layer obstruction.  In
`C_(2m)=<g>`, take `m-1` copies of `g`.  Their subset products
`1,g,...,g^(m-1)` miss `-1=g^m`, and deleting fewer than `m-1` occurrences leaves `g` in
the generated subgroup.  Hence `b=m-1`.

For primes `q=3 mod 4`, the same state embeds in `(Z/4qZ)^*` with its actual element `-1`.
It is compatible with the local unit-group and hard-residue constraints, but no
unconditional construction is known that simultaneously makes all the required shifted
factorizations and the shared `p` prime.  Therefore:

- **proved false:** an absolute escape bound derived layer by layer from finite groups;
- **verified only in range:** `b<=2` on all actual common survivors;
- **unconfirmed:** an absolute bound, or merely a positive proportion of bounded-escape
  layers, for every prime common survivor.

The best next mathematical target is an inverse theorem: classify sequences which miss
`-1` while retaining `-1` in the generated subgroup after two deletions (`b>=3`), then
show that such structured factorizations cannot occur for too many of the shifted
integers `p+4e`.  Lemma 11 can then separate their large prime factors, while a
character-summed sieve handles the small factors.  The present work identifies this
route but does not prove that inverse/cross-shift statement.

## 6. Check against known inverse subset-sum results

The closest standard results found do not directly supply the missing inverse theorem.

- [Savchev--Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0602568) classify long **zero-free** sequences
  in a cyclic group.  Our sequence may have many zero-product subsequences and excludes
  only the prescribed nonzero element `-1`, so their hypothesis does not apply.
- [Bajnok's corrected distinct-class theorem](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548316000092)
  gives subset-sum growth for a generating **set**.  Prime-factor residues here form a
  sequence with multiplicity; the repeated-generator counterexample is precisely outside
  the distinct-set setting.
- [Chang--Chen--Qu--Wang--Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.4492) lower-bound the number of
  representations when a prescribed group element is representable.  The layer miss is
  the opposite branch, where that representation count is zero.

Thus these papers support useful structural analogies, but applying any of them here
would require a new reduction.  No such reduction was verified in this audit.

The continuation [inverse escape and retro-capture](2026-09-04-inverse-escape-and-retro-capture.md)
does obtain a general character-or-sparse-support dichotomy and verifies that all
high-escape individual-layer examples in the tested range are caught by earlier layers.
It does not prove that retro-capture phenomenon in general.
